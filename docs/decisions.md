# Open Icons — decisions and why

Why the set is shaped the way it is.

This is not a changelog. It records **decisions** — the ones where something was
ruled out — so the reasoning survives the commit that carried it, and so a
settled question doesn't get re-opened by accident. Organised by area, not by
date: read it front to back and the system should feel logical.

Each entry carries one metadata line:

```
`date` · `area` · `attribution` · `quality` · `commit`
```

**Quality** is the weight of the change. `reversal` is the one that matters
most: it means this was already tried the other way, and the entry says why it
didn't hold.

| | |
|---|---|
| `minor` | A refinement inside an existing decision. |
| `notable` | A new rule, primitive, or constraint. |
| `structural` | Changes the shape of the system. |
| `reversal` | Undoes a previous call. **Read before re-deciding.** |

**Who decided:**

| | |
|---|---|
| `human` | Matthew's call. Taste, product, or scope. |
| `measured` | The measurement decided it. Neither person nor AI had latitude — the numbers only went one way. |
| `ai` | AI-proposed, human-approved. |

Every entry names what it **ruled out**. If a change ruled nothing out, it is a
fact about the system and belongs in the README or the
[North Star](north-star.md). Some entries add a **Cost** line: the live gotcha
you need to know before you touch that area.

> **Seeded, not complete.** This record starts at the travel pack because that
> is where the repo's first argued calls were written down — they lived only in
> four commit messages until now. The 17 commits before `afbd8d7` describe
> changes rather than argue them, so there is nothing to recover from them.
> Entries below `2026-07-28` are reconstructed from the spec and the proposal.

## Foundations

### The construction rules are derived, not declared

`2026-07-28` · `foundations` · `measured` · `structural` · `e0645a4`

Every number in [`icon-construction-spec.md`](icon-construction-spec.md) was
measured from the 1,185 flattened SVGs in `icons-inspiration/` — ray-cast
thickness sampling, radial silhouette comparison, arc fitting. Where the corpus
is internally inconsistent the spec says so and names the dominant behaviour as
the rule.

**Ruled out:** adopting another system's published spec, or asserting house
rules by taste. Either would have produced a set that looks adjacent to the
inspiration corpus without being generated by the same logic — which is the
failure mode the repo exists to avoid.

**Cost:** the spec's own §2.2 caveat is real. 61% of the 24px corpus lands on no
keyline. Keylines are a strong default for new work, not a fact about the
existing set, and retrofitting them onto old icons is not supported by the
measurement.

### Geometry is a function of stroke width, not a set of coordinates

`2026-07-28` · `foundations` · `human` · `structural` · `e0645a4`

`tools/generate.py` expresses every clearance as a multiple of `W`. The
reference set's clearances are not round numbers that happen to work; they are
the floor `gap ≥ 1 × stroke` solved exactly at `W = 2`.

**Ruled out:** hand-drawn masters. A one-off drawing at `W = 2` cannot move on
the weight axis — change the stroke alone and every clearance collapses at once.
A weight axis is a geometry axis.

**Cost:** you cannot fix an icon by nudging a path. Every fix is a change to the
function that produced it, and the whole set regenerates.

### 24 is the master; 16 is a separate drawing

`2026-07-28` · `foundations` · `measured` · `structural` · `e0645a4`

Best-fit scale mapping 16px silhouettes onto 24px across 572 shared names:
median 1.250, p25 1.219, p75 1.286. A pure rescale would be 1.500. The glyph
tracks its keyline; the stroke does not move at all.

**Ruled out:** exporting one master at two sizes. Scaling 24 → 16 gives a
1.33px stroke that disintegrates at 1×; scaling up gives something too heavy.

**Cost:** no 16px masters exist yet. The 583 files in `icons-inspiration/16` are
reference, not output. This is the largest piece of unbuilt work in the repo.

## Variants

### Fill is a state, not a style

`2026-07-28` · `variants` · `measured` · `structural` · `e0645a4`

> ⚠️ **Overturned 2026-08-04.** See *Line is the default; fill is emphasis*
> below. Kept because it was acted on: it is why `warning-fill` sat unused in a
> banner while `warning` did the alerting, and why the first cut of
> `icon-rules.json` told a consuming AI that fill meant *selected* and nothing
> else.

Line and fill were read as Material's `FILL` axis at its two endpoints, taking
Material's stated semantic: a transition between unfilled and filled — selected
vs unselected, active vs inactive.

**Ruled out:** fill as emphasis, and any intermediate value.

### Line is the default; fill is emphasis

`2026-08-04` · `variants` · `human` · `reversal` · —

**Line is the default icon style.** Almost every icon in almost every surface is
a line icon. **Fill is a style you choose when an icon has to carry more weight
than the words around it** — and the canonical case is status and severity, not
selection. A warning or an error has to be read *before* the sentence beside it,
and a line glyph does not win that race. `warning-fill`, `close-circle-fill`,
`check-circle-fill` and `info-fill` are the alerting forms.

Selection is still a legitimate use of fill; it is simply not the *definition*
of it. Fill is the strongest emphasis step the system has, so the selected item
gets it — one application of the rule rather than the rule itself.

**Ruled out:** "fill is a state, never a style", which is the call this reverses.
It was inherited from Material's axis semantics rather than measured from
anything, and it fails on the most common real use of a filled icon in a
product. Also ruled out: a separate emphasis axis. The system has one emphasis
step and it is the line→fill swap; adding grade to carry severity would be a new
axis for a job fill already does.

**Cost:** fill now means two things — *this is loud* and *this is the one you
are on*. Context separates them cleanly (a banner glyph is not a nav item), but
it does mean "why is this filled?" no longer has a single answer, and a surface
that is both alerting and selectable needs a deliberate call.

**What did not change:** how a fill is *drawn*. The construction rules — never
exceeding the line variant's outer edge, roughly equal total ink, extra mass
spent on counters — are measured and stand. This reversal touched
`data/icon-rules.json`, `docs/north-star.md` and `CLAUDE.md`; it touched neither
`icon-construction-spec.md` nor `construction-tokens.json`, which is the
two-audience split doing its job.

### An icon nothing ever needs to emphasise gets no fill, and that absence is information

`2026-08-03` · `variants` · `ai` · `notable` · `446038a`
*(reworded 2026-08-04 to follow the reversal above — the call itself is unchanged)*

Map chrome (`map`, `layers`, `route`, `compass`) and drawer list markers
(`campfire`, `pets`, `food`, `store`, `wifi`, `shower`) ship line-only. Map
chrome is always a button, never a marker; a list marker in a drawer is never
the loudest thing on the surface.

**Ruled out:** shipping a fill for every icon for the sake of symmetry. A fill
nothing will ever reach for is dead weight, and it implies an emphasis step the
icon does not have.

### An icon built only from open strokes cannot have a fill

`2026-08-03` · `variants` · `measured` · `notable` · `446038a`

`solidify()` on an open run returns the run. `picnic-table` is four bars and has
no interior to flood, so a fill variant would be byte-identical to its line
variant — which the spec's Appendix A already flags as a bug, not a duplicate
(`dock-right-fill`).

**Ruled out:** forcing a fill by thickening the strokes. Stroke width is the one
invariant in the system.

**Cost:** `picnic-table` is a layer-toggle category in Roadtrip and therefore
wants a selected state. It does not have one. If that state is needed it belongs
on the chip, not the glyph.

## Scope

### A third scope, `travel`, sits between generic and product

`2026-07-31` · `scope` · `human` · `structural` · `2e6d4dc`

The README's original model was binary: generic (universal) or product (scoped,
cannot be repurposed). The travel pack fits neither. `campground` is not
universal — it would be noise in a CRM — but it is not Roadtrip-specific either.

**Ruled out:** scoping all 19 travel icons to `roadtrip`. That means the second
travel product redraws them, which is exactly the redundancy this repo exists to
prevent.

**Cost:** nothing enforces the scope at import. It is a recommendation recorded
in [`../data/icon-rules.json`](../data/icon-rules.json) and unratified.

## Selection

### Selection context is structured data, not prose

`2026-08-04` · `selection` · `human` · `structural` · —

The construction rules serve humans and are prose. Which icon to reach for
serves an AI at use time and is per-icon, so it is
[`../data/icon-rules.json`](../data/icon-rules.json), keyed by icon name, with a
cluster tiebreaker naming the single axis that separates each confusable group.

**Ruled out:** documenting selection guidance in the README or the North Star.
Prose cannot be queried, and an AI choosing between `menu` and `more-vertical`
does not need the corner radius.

### The rules file covers confusable clusters only

`2026-08-04` · `selection` · `human` · `notable` · —

39 icons across 12 clusters, against 216 in the set. An entry earns its place by
naming what it rules out; absence means unambiguous, not undocumented.

**Ruled out:** an entry for every icon. Most icons have no twin, so most entries
would be boilerplate — and boilerplate dilutes the signal the file exists to
carry. Also ruled out: full `allowed_contexts` / `disallowed_contexts` arrays
per the README's original sketch, which is a large authoring job whose
maintenance surface grows with every icon added.

**Cost:** the file is generated by `tools/build-icon-rules.py` and validated
against `icons/names.json` at build time. Hand-editing the JSON will be
overwritten and will not be checked.

## The travel pack

### `campsite` and `campground` are two icons, not one

`2026-08-02` · `travel-pack` · `human` · `notable` · `afbd8d7`

`campsite` is one tent, alone, on a ground line — a specific reservable site.
`campground` is that tent plus a second behind it — the whole facility.
Reservability is the line.

**Ruled out:** a single camping icon, and a mirrored duplicate for the second.
A shared baseline with a repeated silhouette reads as an echo, not as *more of
something*.

### `campground`'s second tent is a full tent, not a bare stroke

`2026-08-02` · `travel-pack` · `human` · `reversal` · `afbd8d7`

The draft drew a single diagonal stroke for the second tent. A bare stroke has
no peak of its own, so it reads as a stray line; and its free upper end wants a
round cap while its grounded lower end wants a butt cap. Two rules on one path
is the tell that it was the wrong primitive.

**Ruled out:** the bare stroke, and placing the two tents side by side. Side by
side, holding `gap()` at the ground caps the far tent at ~4 units wide and
forces the near one to narrow until its own door falls under the clearance
floor. The far tent is occluded by the near one's silhouette dilated by
`moat()` — the same answer `photo-stack` and `people` already use.

### Sharp vertices are not allowed, including on a tent

`2026-08-02` · `travel-pack` · `measured` · `reversal` · `afbd8d7`

An earlier pass tried `ngon()` on the tent, saw the linter score worsen, and
reverted with the rationalisation that "the base has to stay flush and sharp".
That was wrong: the lint error `stroke mode 3.225 != 2.0` **was** the
sharp-corner symptom. §4.2 puts every exterior corner on a radius and the set
has no sharp-vertex allowance. Rebuilt through `ngon()`; campsite's score went
59 → 9.

**Ruled out:** the rationalisation. Recorded because the failure mode — reading a
symptom as a false positive and reverting a correct fix — is repeatable.

### Apex extent scales from `warning`, not from a literal radius

`2026-08-02` · `travel-pack` · `measured` · `notable` · `afbd8d7`

Solving `e = r / tan(θ/2)` for a literal `r = 2` over-rounds: the extent blows up
as an apex sharpens, and on a tent it pulled the peak ~3 units below its own
vertex. `warning`'s triangle is the only shipped sharp vertex, so its ratio
(`TRI_E / 14`) is the family constant and every pointed form takes its extent
from its own height.

**Ruled out:** one flat extent per polygon, and the literal-radius solve. A
polygon whose vertices differ in angle needs a different extent at each one —
which is what `ngon_r()` and `e_for_r()` exist for.

### `mountain` is one silhouette with a deep saddle

`2026-08-03` · `travel-pack` · `measured` · `notable` · `446038a`

Three separators from `campground`, all structural: one closed outline with a
saddle rather than two moated tents; the horizontal-rect keyline 20×16 against
campsite's square 18×18; and a counter at the peak (a snow line) rather than at
the base (a doorway). The saddle sits 9.8 below the main peak because a shallow
one crowds the snow line up until the counter above it has 0.55 inradius, under
the §7 floor of 1.0. Shipped it measures 1.275.

**Ruled out:** two overlapping triangles, which is `campground`'s construction
and would have been the same icon at 16px.

### `park` is a broadleaf canopy, not a notched gable

`2026-08-03` · `travel-pack` · `measured` · `reversal` · `446038a`

The proposal specified a notched gable plus trunk — a conifer. Two tiers put the
underside of the upper slope 2.29 from the lower one, so the step closes at `W`.
One tier is just `campsite` wearing a trunk.

**Ruled out:** the conifer. A canopy also settles the proposal's open question 3
(`park` vs `mountain` at 16px) by not being a triangle at all — which mattered
because the A/B render that question asked for could not be produced.

### `trailer` does not draw the tow vehicle

`2026-08-03` · `travel-pack` · `human` · `reversal` · `2f6d567`

The proposal argued a trailer without a tow vehicle is just a box. It is the
tongue that says trailer.

**Ruled out:** drawing both vehicles. Two vehicles inside 20 units gives each
about 8, at which point neither is legible.

### The vehicle family shares one chassis and one fill counter

`2026-08-03` · `travel-pack` · `ai` · `notable` · `2f6d567`

`car`, `rv` and `trailer` share a wheel datum lifted from `cart` (solid marks of
`r_mark()` sitting `gapm()` under the body's ink) and a belt-line crease as
their fill counter.

**Ruled out:** a window as the fill counter. The coach interior is 9 tall, a
window needs `gap()` above and below, and what is left is 5 of ink around a
3-unit opening — under the floor before it is drawn.

### `fuel` and `ev-charger` are one machine

`2026-08-03` · `travel-pack` · `ai` · `notable` · `2f6d567`

Same tank, plinth and arm geometry; the face mark and what the arm ends in are
the only difference. That the two are the same kind of object is true by
construction rather than by eye.

**Ruled out:** two independent drawings. `ev-charger`'s line variant is
byte-identical across the refactor, which is the check that it was
behaviour-preserving.

## Usage

Calls about *when and whether* to use an icon, rather than how one is drawn.
All five were made 2026-08-04 and are recorded in plain language in
[`principles.md`](principles.md).

### An icon is not always the answer

`2026-08-04` · `usage` · `human` · `structural` · —

Text is frequently clearer than a symbol, and icons added for their own sake are
noise: a screen where everything carries a glyph has no emphasis left to spend.
The test is whether someone would understand it faster with just the word.

**Ruled out:** icon-by-default — the habit of giving every action a mark because
the row looks unfinished without one. Also ruled out: drawing for abstract
concepts (compliance, eligibility, entitlement). Those have no shape, and the
attempt produces a symbol nobody can read.

### Icons work by convention, not invention

`2026-08-04` · `usage` · `human` · `notable` · —

An icon is recognised from memory, not deduced. The set that works reliably
without a label is small and already learned: overflow, disclosure, navigation,
and a handful of concrete objects. Use icons where the convention is doing the
work.

**Ruled out:** teaching a new symbol inside one product. People arrive with the
vocabulary they have, not a willingness to learn ours. An invented mark needs a
label — at which point principle 1 asks whether it is earning its space.

### Meaning is bound by the product, not by the library

`2026-08-04` · `usage` · `human` · `structural` · —

The library's job is that there is **one good drawing of each thing**. It is not
that each drawing has exactly one meaning. One icon often carries several — a
plus is add, create, invite and expand — and several icons can be defensible for
one action, where remove might be an X, a minus or a trash can depending on what
happens next.

That ambiguity is resolved in a **product manifest**: in this product, this
action uses this icon. `data/icon-rules.json` carries the shape and a seeded
Roadtrip example built from the fourteen emoji and eleven text glyphs the app
ships as controls today.

**Ruled out:** a one-to-one icon↔meaning map, and deleting a drawing because two
of them could serve one action. Both push the ambiguity back into the library,
which is the one place it cannot be answered — the answer depends on a product's
own flows.

**Cost:** an unbound product has no answer at all. This trades "the library
decides" for "the product must decide", and a product that skips the manifest is
worse off than before.

### Never brand an icon for a feature

`2026-08-04` · `usage` · `human` · `notable` · —

Icons name actions, not features. The failure pattern: a feature launches, needs
a mark, and adopts an abstract icon as its badge — a sparkle becoming "AI", or
"new", or "premium".

**Ruled out:** feature-badging an icon that already has literal uses. Sparkle
already meant clean-this-up and highlight-this; once it is a feature's mark those
uses read as promotion, and every screen that used it innocently starts to look
like an ad. Features get renamed, repositioned and retired; the icon outlives all
of it.

### Cultural semantics is an open hazard list, not a cleared one

`2026-08-04` · `usage` · `human` · `notable` · —

A mark does not mean the same thing everywhere. `data/icon-rules.json` now
carries the hazards known in *this* set: maru/batsu (in Japan a circle means
correct and a cross means wrong, against the Western check-for-correct and
circle-as-neutral), RTL mirroring across the 53 directional icons, region-bound
objects like mailboxes and currency, and a standing note to keep hands and
gestures out of the set.

**Ruled out:** shipping the set as though it were culturally neutral, and
equally, blocking on a full locale review before anything ships. The middle
position is that the hazards are written down and any icon encoding correctness,
direction, a hand or a physical object is marked **unverified** outside a
Western context.

**Cost:** "unverified" is not "safe". This records the risk; it does not retire
it.

## Metadata

### The per-icon layer is one generated file, with a validator that fails the build

`2026-08-04` · `metadata` · `human` · `structural` · —

`data/icon-metadata.json` carries description, keywords, category, naming class,
RTL direction, status, `deprecated_by`, `since` and `has_fill` for all 152 line
icons. Built by `tools/build-icon-metadata.py` from family rules plus per-icon
overrides, with the derived fields read from `names.json` rather than typed.

The validator is the load-bearing part. It **exits non-zero** if any icon lacks
a description or a keyword, if an alias or a `deprecated_by` points at something
that does not exist, or if a direction value is outside the vocabulary. Adding an
icon without adding its metadata is a build error.

**Ruled out:** Polaris's one-YAML-per-icon model. It suits a set that is drawn;
ours is generated, and 152 sidecars would be 152 places for the same field to
drift. Also ruled out: making metadata optional, which is the same as making it
absent — a file that is allowed to be partly missing is one nobody can build on.

**Cost:** a new icon is now two edits, not one. That is the intended cost.

### Aliases are metadata, not files

`2026-08-04` · `metadata` · `human` · `notable` · —

58 alternate names resolve to real icons: `zoom-in` → `search-add`, `zoom-out` →
`search-minus`, `drag-handle` → `more-vertical`, plus the words people actually
type — `hamburger`, `kebab`, `cog`, `bin`, `tick`, `magnifier`.

This closes the alias question the travel-pack proposal raised and answers it the
way the proposal wanted: `search-add` and `search-minus` **are** zoom-in and
zoom-out, and no second drawing exists.

**Ruled out:** emitting duplicate SVG files under the alias names. That is two
drawings of one thing — principle 6 — and it would put the redundancy back into
the library to save consumers a lookup. Also ruled out: an alias table in
`export.py`, which would have made aliases a build-output concern rather than a
fact about an icon.

### RTL direction is measured where it can be, and admitted where it cannot

`2026-08-04` · `metadata` · `measured` · `notable` · —

The `direction` field takes Fluent's vocabulary — `mirror` | `none` | `unique` —
plus **`unreviewed`**, which is the default and is deliberately *not* a synonym
for `none`.

Of 152 icons: 23 `mirror` (a human call on the horizontal directional families),
68 `none`, and 61 still `unreviewed`. **43 of the 68 are measured rather than
judged**: an icon whose ink is symmetric about x=12 cannot change under a
horizontal mirror, so `none` is a fact about the drawing. `data/measured-symmetry.json`
holds the sampling result, and the build fails if anything declares `mirror`
while measuring symmetric.

**Ruled out:** defaulting unknown icons to `none`. That is the failure this
field exists to prevent — silence reading as a decision, and 61 unexamined icons
shipping as though someone had checked them.

**Cost:** 61 icons still have no answer, and the metadata now says so out loud
rather than looking complete.

## Tooling

### `icon-lint.py` cannot score generated output

`2026-08-03` · `tooling` · `measured` · `notable` · `446038a`

The linter expects flattened SVGs and ray-casts filled contours, so it reads a
stroke-attributed path as a solid. `add` and `close` — the spec's own canonical
examples — score 100 "unmeasurable"; `warning` scores 67; `bolt` scores
identically to `bolt-fill`.

**Ruled out:** using lint scores as a conformance signal for anything
`generate.py` produces. Verification is geometric measurement instead —
bounding box, per-element clearance, largest-inscribed-disc per counter, and a
regression diff of `icons/icons.json` against `HEAD`.

**Cost:** it also writes `tools/lint.json` into the repo as a side effect, and
defaults `ICON_ROOT` to `icons-inspiration/` rather than `icons/`.
